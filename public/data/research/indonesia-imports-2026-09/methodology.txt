Searching for H200 and B300 AI clusters in Indonesia imports
Research note dated 23 September 2026

SOURCE AND SCOPE
Two supplied spreadsheet exports, each with one Data worksheet:
- indonesias-imports-from-world-by-product_8471 (3).xlsx: monthly,
  January 2025 through July 2026.
- indonesias-imports-from-world-by-product_8471 (2).xlsx: quarterly,
  Q1 2025 through Q2 2026. Its 202501-202504 and 202601-202602 headers
  represent quarters in this export, as confirmed by monthly reconciliation.

Reporter: 360, Indonesia. Partner: 000, World. Original values: US$ thousands.
These supplied files have not been independently authenticated against the
original issuing data provider. Internal reconciliation checks consistency;
it does not establish the accuracy or provenance of the underlying records.
The downloads here are derived data, not authenticated customs declarations.

monthly.csv retains the monthly HS 8471 total and the selected HS6 categories
847150, 847149 and 847180. categories.csv compares all eight HS6 categories
and the parent 8471 total. Columns marked USDm are US$ millions.
The monthly reconciliation column is in US$ thousands and compares the sum
of all eight source subcategories with the parent total, not only the three
selected subcategories included in monthly.csv.

Do not add the parent HS 8471 row to its component categories. Do not combine
the quarterly and monthly exports as if they were separate imports.
All 54 category-quarter comparisons agree within US$1,000; monthly sums of
the eight subcategories agree with the parent within US$2,000, consistent
with rounding. Comparisons below use January-July in both years.

ILLUSTRATIVE ALLOCATION
Assume 50% of HS 847150 and 35% of HS 847149 are AI computing equipment:
2,850.872 x 0.50 + 1,090.499 x 0.35 = US$1,807.11065 million,
rounded to US$1.8 billion in the note.

The 50% and 35% shares are analyst-selected assumptions. They are not
estimated from shipment descriptions, fitted probabilities, or verified
allocations. These broad categories also cover conventional servers,
workstations and industrial computers. The resulting estimate concerns
equipment value, not GPU-chip value or the total cost of an AI factory.
It does not include a separately estimated amount for power, cooling,
networking or construction.

LIMITS OF THE INFERENCE
These aggregate workbooks contain no importer names, counterparties,
product models, part numbers or installation status. They cannot establish
that any specific Indonesian company imported NVIDIA H200 or B300 systems, or that
the equipment is installed or operational. No HS code here is exclusive to
H200 or B300. Zero imports of either model remains compatible with this
evidence alone.
Company/model attribution requires shipment-level documents specifying
importer, date, model or part number, quantity and declared value.

The earlier social-media chart of Taiwan chip exports is a different series
with an unverified HS filter; it is not used in these calculations.

HS BREAKDOWN AND CLUSTER RESEARCH FRAME
The study asks whether part of the import growth represents H200- or
B300-based GPU clusters for AI token factories (inference infrastructure)
or AI data centers. It identifies categories and periods to investigate;
it has not identified a verified GPU model, cluster, operator or site.

HS 8471 is the parent category, not an additional line to add to its children.
The table in the article is rendered from categories.csv. Descriptions are
simplified from the WCO HS 2022 nomenclature at the six-digit level:
- 847150: processing units outside 847141/847149; may incorporate storage/I/O.
- 847149: computers presented as systems.
- 847130: portable computers up to 10 kg, with processor, keyboard and display.
- 847141: computers with processing and input/output in the same housing.
- 847160: input/output peripherals.
- 847170: data-storage units.
- 847180: other computer units.
- 847190: remaining goods within the heading.
The article prioritizes 847150 and 847149 as candidate search pools for
GPU-server shipments. This is our research interpretation, not an official
AI-specific classification. Processing units are not synonymous with GPU
chips; a system declaration does not prove an entire cluster was imported.
Actual classifications depend on the goods as presented and the relevant
customs declaration, including Indonesia's more detailed tariff lines.

Additional codes to investigate, with NO values in the supplied dataset:
854231 (processor/controller integrated circuits), 847330 (computer parts
and accessories), and 851762 (data communication, including switching and
routing equipment). These are not assigned H200/B300 classifications.
Bare chips, populated boards, servers and systems are different forms.
These additional categories are not included in the scenario calculation.

TIMING CALCULATIONS (US$ MILLIONS)
February 2026 systems: 683.418 / 1,090.499 = 62.6702% of Jan-Jul systems.
May-Jul 2026 processing units: 654.453 + 852.058 + 661.632 = 2,168.143.
2,168.143 / 2,850.872 = 76.0519% of Jan-Jul processing units.
Processing-unit growth: 2,850.872 - 1,072.631 = 1,778.241.
1,778.241 / (5,255.474 - 2,773.194) = 71.6374% of HS 8471 growth.
Combined growth: (3,941.371 / 1,610.584 - 1) x 100 = 144.717% (about 145%).
These are concentrations in time, not proof that deliveries belong to the
same project. Import values can change with prices and equipment mix.

GPU CLUSTERS AND LIMITS
NVIDIA's reference architecture describes an HGX H200 configuration with
32 eight-GPU servers (256 GPUs). Its DGX B300 guide describes an eight-B300
system with CPUs, memory, storage and network interfaces. These examples
explain why assembled systems matter; they are not Indonesian shipment
records. The US$1,807.11065m allocation is for potential AI computing
equipment, not only GPU chips. It cannot yield a model split, GPU/cluster
count, tokens per second, utilization, revenue or operating capacity.

Connecting the signal to a cluster requires model/part-number evidence,
shipment quantities and values, importer/consignee and destination matching,
and separate installation/commissioning and workload evidence. Distributor
imports do not identify the eventual operator. Avoid counting components
inside a complete system again as separate equipment.
